## Summary

Dario Amodei argues for deliberately pacing AI development to ensure safety keeps up with rapidly advancing capabilities, citing risks like recursive self-improvement and misaligned agent swarms.

**Core argument**
- AI offers transformative benefits (e.g., curing diseases, economic growth) but poses existential risks (loss of control, cyberattacks, bioterrorism).
- Recent incidents (e.g., OpenAI-Hugging Face swarm) and recursive self-improvement demand urgent caution.
- Pacing (not halting) AI progress is necessary to align safety with capability growth.

**Proposed pacing framework**
- **Embedded Evaluators**: Third-party teams (e.g., METR) get employee-like access to audit safety practices, report incidents, and verify alignment.
- **Democratic Coordination**: US/EU frontier labs agree on safety standards and limits on unchecked progress, with government support.
- **Global Coordination**: US and allies attempt limited agreements with authoritarian regimes (e.g., banning AI bioweapons, testing models pre-release).

**Why pacing helps**
- **Operational Excellence**: More time to fix execution failures (e.g., reinforcement learning environment filtering).
- **Alignment**: Improve techniques to prevent rare, unexpected misbehavior in advanced models.
- **Interpretability**: Advance methods to understand model internals (e.g., detecting unverbalized motivations).
- **Testing/Evaluation**: Develop robust evaluations to catch deception in highly capable models.

**Key constraints**
- Pacing must preserve democracies’ AI lead over autocracies (e.g., China) to avoid geopolitical risks.
- Measures to maintain lead: restrict chip sales to China, prevent model theft, and crack down on unauthorized distillation.
- Global agreements are hardest; start with narrow bans (e.g., AI bioweapons) before attempting broader pacing.

**Anthropic’s commitments**
- Unilaterally adopting embedded evaluators with full access (offices, tools, publishing rights).
- Advocating for US regulation to formalize embedded evaluators and safety standards.
