## Summary

Claude autonomously produced the first complete, computer-verified formal proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem in Lean, demonstrating a major leap in AI-driven mathematical formalization.

**Achievement**
- First end-to-end, computer-checked proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem (FLT) generated by AI (Claude) in 11 days.
- Proof consists of 13 million lines of Lean code and 29,500 intermediate theorems.
- Verified by Lean using only its three standard axioms, with no additional assumptions.

**Technical Approach**
- Used Prove2Me, an open collaborative platform for formalization, to manage theorem dependencies via a directed acyclic graph (DAG).
- Multi-agent collaboration: dozens of Claude agents worked in parallel, defining concepts and proving intermediate theorems.
- Followed a simplified version of Wiles’s proof (via Darmon, Diamond, and Taylor).
- Human input limited to high-level guidance (e.g., prioritizing specific theorems like Jacobian as a scheme).

**Impact and Implications**
- Demonstrates feasibility of large-scale autoformalization of complex mathematical proofs.
- Potential to reduce the burden of verifying new mathematical results, which can take years.
- Formalization may become a standard companion to human-readable proofs, especially for AI-generated work.
- Enables rigorous checking of LLM-produced mathematics, addressing trust and correctness concerns.

**Performance and Scale**
- Proof is over 5x larger than Mathlib, the principal Lean library of formalized mathematics.
- Consumed ~6 billion output tokens from a model comparable to Claude Fable 5.1.
- Initial failed attempts contributed ~7% of non-boilerplate lines to the final proof.

**Broader Context**
- FLT’s original proof by Wiles (1995) was 129 pages and took months to verify.
- Formalization starts from axioms, requiring explicit steps that human proofs often omit.
- Previous community efforts (e.g., Imperial College London’s FLT project) estimated years for completion.
- Success suggests collaborative AI formalization of major theorems is achievable with current tools.

**Future Directions**
- Anthropic and others are supporting external researchers with grants and resources for formalization projects.
- Goal: expand formalization to other major theorems and improve tools like Lean and Mathlib.
- Formalization seen as a trust-building measure for AI-assisted mathematical research.
