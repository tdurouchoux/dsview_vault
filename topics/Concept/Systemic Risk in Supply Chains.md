Systemic risk in supply chains refers to the potential for a disruption at one or more critical nodes (e.g., suppliers, transportation routes) to cascade through the network, leading to widespread inefficiencies, shortages, or failures. This concept involves analyzing how localized disruptions (e.g., geopolitical events, natural disasters) can amplify into global shortages, price volatility, or systemic collapse. Tools like stress testing, scenario analysis, and network modeling are often used to assess and mitigate such risks.
