The data-ink ratio is a principle in data visualization introduced by Edward Tufte. It refers to the proportion of a graphic's ink that is used to present data compared to the total ink used in the graphic. The goal is to maximize the data-ink ratio by eliminating non-data ink (e.g., excessive gridlines, decorative elements, or redundant labels) and redundant data ink (e.g., duplicative labels or chartjunk). This principle emphasizes clarity and efficiency in visual communication, ensuring that the visual representation of data is as clear and uncluttered as possible.
